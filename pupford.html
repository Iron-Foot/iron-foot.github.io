01 — Overview

A brand that has to be warm on the shelf and sharp in the feed.

Pupford sells food and training treats straight to pup parents who are two scrolls away from a competitor. That splits the design work in half: the brand side has to feel hand-drawn, warm and genuinely fond of pups, while the performance side has to survive a paid feed where the first half-second decides everything.

Working in-house meant owning both ends of the same system — art directing and shooting the campaigns, cutting the ads out of that same library, and turning the illustration set loose across social, email and print.

  1. 01

    Campaign photography

    Five shoot days — Summer BBQ, the peanut butter set build, the pink and flower studio series, and product. Concept, styling, shooting and retouching.

  2. 02

    Product photography

    Clean packshots of the whole Good Dog Food and Train ranges, framed identically so flavour variants read as one system.

  3. 03

    Landing pages

    Long-form Good Dog Food builds with tested angles — a product-led desktop page, a nutrition-authority variant, and a mobile listicle.

  4. 04

    Paid social & static ads

    Ad sets built as sequences and as colourway families, so a winning concept can be re-cut for a new audience without a new shoot.

  5. 05

    Illustration system

    An original cast of hand-drawn pups — Procreate, Dry Ink brush, always on Chiffon Cream — running through packaging, social and email.

  6. 06

    Editorial carousels & email

    Educational carousels, a twelve-part recipe series, and lifecycle email built on the same illustrated language.

Conversion

Testing an angle should cost a layout, not a shoot.

The desktop build leads with the product and the promise. The nutrition build leans on ingredients and authority for a colder audience. The mobile listicle reframes the whole thing as an article — the reader is halfway down before it asks for anything.

All three are cut from the same photo library and the same illustration set, so a new argument is a day of layout rather than a new production.
